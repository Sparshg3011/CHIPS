# CHIPS: pitch script

**CHIPS** stands for **Cloud and Hardware Integration Platform for Small Software**.

Narration for the animated film at `/pitch/`, scene by scene. Useful as speaker notes if you present live.

## Title. CHIPS

CHIPS, the Cloud and Hardware Integration Platform for Small Software. It’s an agentic forward deployed engineer: a layer between software vendors and their customers that handles discovery, customization, deployment, integration and support.

## Act 1. The customer need

Meet Cocoa&Co., a growing bakery that built a website and immediately got flooded with orders. They desperately need data tools to track profit, margins, and bestsellers, but don’t know where to turn.

## Act 2. The seller bottleneck

On the other side, we have DataVis, a 2-person analytics startup with world-class visualization tools. They have great tech, but zero bandwidth to negotiate, scope custom integrations, or tailor products for non-technical buyers.

## Act 3. Who we serve & the evidence

We serve two customers: small businesses whose workflow doesn’t fit off-the-shelf software, and small software teams with no services team. In Capterra’s October 2025 survey of 3,385 buyers, 66% hit regret or disruption, mostly during implementation. Big companies fix this with forward deployed engineers, a role whose postings grew 800% in 2025. Small vendors can’t.

## Act 4. A fourth option

Today a team like Cocoa&Co. gets three options: adapt to generic SaaS, pay for custom engineering, or build and maintain internal tools. CHIPS adds a fourth: a reusable product, adapted to each customer’s actual workflow.

## Act 5. Discovery & matchmaking

That’s where CHIPS comes in. Cocoa&Co. submits their query, our system reasons over their exact operational needs, and automatically matches them with DataVis.

## Act 6. Agent deployment & sandbox

We deploy an AI agent into DataVis’s codebase. It learns the tools and spins up a dedicated playground where Cocoa&Co. can chat, test interactive software mocks, and select only the 3 specific services they need.

## Act 7. Scoping & handshake

The agent handles technical negotiations and outputs a complete CSRS specification for quick human verification and pricing. The deal is closed seamlessly without consuming engineering hours.

## Act 8. Integration & fulfillment

Finally, post-deal fulfillment is automated. Whether modifying the seller’s dashboard view or directly embedding MCP or API integrations into the buyer’s codebase, our agent handles the setup end to end.

## Act 9. After go-live

After launch, the agent stays on both sides. It turns Cocoa&Co.’s change requests into tested configuration, checks every customization against new DataVis releases, and flags repeated requests that should become core features.

## Act 10. The stack

The small-software cloud makes customization technically safe. The agent makes it operationally accessible. The unit of work becomes a workflow for one customer, built on the seller’s product and kept compatible through every upgrade.

## Act 11. Why we win

Every alternative solves only part of this. Marketplaces stop at the sale. Agencies write custom code that forks. AI app builders start from scratch, and you maintain it. Human forward deployed engineers work, but only for six-figure contracts. We customize the vendor’s own product, test every change, and carry it through upgrades, on a bakery’s budget.

## Act 12. Business model

Who pays? Sellers pay a 15% success fee on a deal’s first year, only when it closes. Each live customization adds a $49 monthly care fee to the buyer’s price. On a $300-a-month plan, Cocoa&Co. pays $4,188 in year one, DataVis keeps $3,060 it would have turned away, and we earn $1,128, with zero engineering hours spent.

## Act 13. The opportunity

That makes small deals economical on both sides, and opens a long tail of specialized software businesses too small to run their own sales engineering, professional services and customer success teams.

## Act 14. First 100 customers

Our first 100 customers come in three steps. First, we sign ten small software vendors as design partners, and run their first deals ourselves, concierge style. Second, each vendor hands us the requests they’ve been turning away. Ten each makes a hundred buyers. Third, we list on marketplaces small businesses already use, starting with the Shopify App Store.

## Act 15. Prototype & what’s next

Our clickable prototype runs the full loop: intake, matching, a live sandbox, an auto-generated spec, deployment and support. Try it yourself. Next come 20 buyer and 20 vendor interviews, then three concierge pilots, and our first paid deployments within 90 days, measuring time to go-live and requests handled without an engineer.

## Close. The close

The winning product won’t promise arbitrary changes. It creates a constrained environment where a large, useful class of changes can be made safely, tested automatically, and carried forward through upgrades.
